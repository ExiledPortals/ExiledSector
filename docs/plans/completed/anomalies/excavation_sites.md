# Anomaly: Excavation Sites

Some of the chart's planets hide bolt-holes: sealed Domain-era caverns under the crust. Each site holds its own loot and one way in, rolled 50/50 when the chart is made: an open shaft with a fleet nesting below, which only frigates and destroyers can follow down, or a collapsed shaft that only marines can get through. Either is optional; you can leave a site alone.

Status: built 2026-10-28 (package `exiledsector.charts.anomaly.excavation`), not yet checked in game. Reworked 2026-10-28 from the dig-and-scale-the-boss trade: each site now has its own loot and its own encounter, and nothing touches the boss. See `../../anomalies/overview.md` for the shared framework.

## Lore

During the AI Wars, frontier garrisons that couldn't hold orbit dug bolt-holes: hardened caverns under the crust, reached by a single bored shaft, where a picket of light hulls could wait out a bombardment. The shafts were cut no wider than a destroyer's beam, so nothing big enough to threaten the garrison could follow it down. Some shafts are still open, with something nesting in the dark below; others caved in long ago, and only a marine team on foot can get through the rubble.

## How it plays

The chart marks one to three planets with an excavation site (placeholder count), shown in the chart's intel and the planet's dialog. Interacting with the planet offers the site, says which way in it has and hints at what it holds (a leaning toward one `DropType`, placeholder), and lets you go in or leave. A site you clear is spent; one you leave stays until the run closes.

### Open shaft: the fight below

A fleet of the chart's faction holds the cavern, or a derelict picket still standing watch (placeholder split). It is a temporary fleet that is never on the map, started from the planet's dialog like the Anomalous Debris' dormant fleets, with `DormantEncounters`-style config: no pulled-in allies, enemies or stations, and no AI retreat, so the autoresolve pursuit option never comes up.

Only frigates and destroyers fight on either side. The enemy fleet is generated with `FleetParamsV3.maxShipSize = 2`. On your side, your cruisers and capitals hold station over the shaft mouth: a battle plugin removes them from your reserves when the battle starts (`CombatFleetManagerAPI.removeFromReserves`), and does the same for the enemy as a safeguard. A removed ship lands in none of the engagement result's lists, so it takes no CR loss or damage and is never pursued. Going in is greyed out, with a tooltip saying why, when you have no frigate or destroyer that can deploy.

The battle is cramped: a custom `BattleCreationPlugin` (picked through `CampaignPlugin.pickBattleCreationPlugin` for the site's fleet) gives a small map, a dark cavern background and rock fields along the edges, with no objectives.

Winning drops the site's loot through the normal fleet loot path (`ChartFleetLoot`, `ChartVanillaLoot`), sized from the site's own budget rather than the fleet's strength.

### Collapsed shaft: the ground operation

The site uses vanilla's non-market raid (`MarketCMD` `raidNonMarket`, set up through `$raidDifficulty`, `$raidGoBackTrigger` and `$raidContinueTrigger`, as `GADataFromRuins` and `RaidSecretOutpostMission` do on planets with no colony). That brings vanilla's raid screen, marine losses and XP, Ground Support and story-point options with it. The difficulty scales with tier, and the site's loot goes to the raid's cargo through `ChartCargoLoot`, scaled by the raid's effectiveness.

Built with `raid.ChartRaid` instead (decided while building): it is the Supply Convoy's copy of exactly that non-market raid (raid screen, ground support, marine losses and XP, spoils screen), opened straight from our dialog, while vanilla's `raidNonMarket` only runs inside a rule-based dialog with `rules.csv` rows for its triggers. Vanilla's one story-point raid option, the secret raid, is offered only for real markets, so a non-market raid loses nothing.

## Chart modifiers

Tuning lines are chart effects carried by an atlas source, per `overview.md`:

- `CHART_EXTRA_EXCAVATION_SITES`: +N to excavation sites.
- `CHART_EXCAVATION_LOOT_QUANTITY_PERCENT`: increased quantity of items found at excavation sites.

## Open questions

- Check in game that the opening deployment screen never shows the removed cruisers and capitals. Reserves are filled in `CombatFleetManager`'s constructor, before combat plugins' `init`, so removing them there should be early enough. Built so they should never get there: the cavern map hands only frigates and destroyers to the loader, and the plugin's removal is the safeguard.
- Check in game how a battle plays when your flagship is a cruiser or capital and stays above: you start with no ship to pilot and transfer command to a deployed one.
- The engine splits the battle's deployment points between the sides by fleet strength, so a fleet heavy with capitals may shrink the enemy's share. If it does, recompute both caps from frigates and destroyers only, as `ChartBattlePlugin` already rewrites them. Still open: the large hulls never reach the loader, but whether the split reads the loader or the campaign fleets needs the in-game check.
- Ships another mod spawns mid-battle bypass the reserves; decide whether the plugin should also retreat any cruiser or capital that appears. Partly answered: a large hull put back into the reserves is removed again within a second; one spawned straight onto the field is left alone for now.
- Whether going in costs anything beyond the fight or the marines (supplies or heavy machinery to break the seal). Not built: going in costs nothing.
- Check in game that the cavern map is picked: `pickBattleCreationPlugin` receives either the shaft fleet or the battle's combined fleet, and both are matched, but which one the engine passes is unconfirmed.
- Check in game that losing or leaving keeps the fleet with its losses for the next attempt, and that winning drops the site's vanilla drops and items on the salvage screen.
